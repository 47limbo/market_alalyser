    What the chart shows

    Each marking below matches its colour on the chart. None of them is a signal on its own: traders look for several lining up (the Entry Check does this for you) and always plan the stop first.

    Market structure

    ▲ BOS▼ BOSBreak of structure

    What it is. A candle body closes beyond the last swing high (bullish, green) or swing low (bearish, red) in the direction of the current trend. The line runs from the swing that was broken to the candle that broke it.

    Effect. Confirms the trend is continuing: buyers (or sellers) were strong enough to take out the previous extreme.

    How to use it. Trade in the direction of recent BOS. After a bullish BOS, wait for a pullback into a gap or order block to buy, rather than chasing the break.

    ▲ CHoCH▼ CHoCHChange of character

    What it is. The first break against the trend: a close above the last lower high in a downtrend, or below the last higher low in an uptrend. Drawn with a dashed line.

    Effect. An early warning that the trend may be reversing. It is weaker than a BOS until price confirms it with a further break the same way.

    How to use it. Stop adding to the old trend. A CHoCH that follows a liquidity sweep and comes with displacement is the core reversal signal the Entry Check looks for.

    Displacement

    What it is. A large, decisive candle: body at least 1.5 times the average range (ATR) with small wicks. Not drawn separately; it shows in the Entry Check and the event list.

    Effect. Shows real intent from big participants and usually leaves a fair value gap behind.

    How to use it. Trust breaks that come with displacement far more than slow, overlapping drifts through a level.

    Liquidity

    BSLSSLBuy-side and sell-side liquidity

    What it is. Dotted amber lines at swing highs (BSL) and swing lows (SSL) that price has not yet traded through. Stop orders and breakout orders cluster just beyond them.

    Effect. Price is drawn towards these pools because that is where large orders can be filled.

    How to use it. Use the nearest untouched pool in your direction as a target, and keep your own stop away from obvious pools.

    EQHEQLEqual highs and lows

    What it is. Two swing highs (or lows) within a tenth of an ATR of each other: an obvious double top or bottom.

    Effect. Many traders put stops right behind them, so they are a favourite place for a stop hunt.

    How to use it. Expect them to be swept before a real move. Do not treat equal highs as strong resistance to short against.

    PDHAsia lowPrevious day and Asia session levels

    What it is. Previous day high and low (PDH, PDL, by UTC day) and the high and low of the Asia session (8pm to midnight New York time).

    Effect. Levels the whole market watches. London and New York often sweep one side of the Asia range or the previous day before the day's main move.

    How to use it. Watch for a sweep of one of these early in London or New York as a setup trigger, and use the opposite one as a target.

    ▲▼Liquidity sweep

    What it is. Price wicks through a liquidity level but closes back inside within three candles. A green up arrow means sell-side was taken under a low; a red down arrow means buy-side was taken above a high.

    Effect. Stops have been triggered and filled by larger players, which often marks a turning point.

    How to use it. A sweep alone is not an entry. Wait for a CHoCH or BOS the other way, then enter on the pullback, with your stop beyond the sweep's wick.

    Price zones

    Fair value gap (FVG)

    What it is. A three-candle gap where the first candle's high and third candle's low do not overlap (bullish, green box) or the reverse (bearish, red box). Price moved too fast to trade there.

    Effect. Price often comes back to fill part of the gap, then continues in the direction of the move. The middle of the gap is a common reaction point.

    How to use it. After a break, place a limit entry in the gap (the app uses its midpoint). If price closes all the way through it, the gap has failed and flips to work the other way.

    Order block (OB)

    What it is. The last opposite-coloured candle before a displacement move that broke structure: the last down candle before a rally (blue) or the last up candle before a drop (orange).

    Effect. Where large orders were placed; price often reacts when it returns there.

    How to use it. Use it as an entry zone when there is no gap, with the stop just beyond the block. A close through it turns it into a breaker (dashed outline, shown with "Show filled and broken zones").

    EQ 50%Premium and discount

    What it is. The current dealing range, from the latest swing low to swing high, split at 50%. The top half (light red) is premium; the bottom half (light green) is discount.

    Effect. Professionals prefer to buy cheap and sell expensive inside a range.

    How to use it. Look for longs in discount and shorts in premium. A long taken in premium has a worse stop and less room to target.

    OTEOptimal trade entry

    What it is. The 62% to 79% pullback of the dealing range, drawn as a dashed cyan box.

    Effect. A deep but not complete pullback, where many trend continuations restart.

    How to use it. An FVG or order block inside the OTE box is a higher-quality entry than one outside it.

    Volume and time

    VWAPVWAP and bands

    What it is. The average price paid today, weighted by volume, resetting each UTC day (solid purple). Dashed and dotted lines are 1 and 2 standard deviations away.

    Effect. Intraday traders treat it as fair value: above it buyers are in control, below it sellers are. The outer bands mark stretched moves.

    How to use it. Favour longs above VWAP and shorts below. Moves to the 2 standard deviation band often snap back towards VWAP.

    VAHVolume profile

    What it is. Bars on the left edge showing how much volume traded at each price over the last 200 candles. The darkest bar is the point of control (POC); VAH and VAL bound the 70% of volume around it.

    Effect. High-volume prices act like magnets and support or resistance; thin areas get crossed quickly.

    How to use it. Expect price to slow down at the POC and value area edges, and to move fast through gaps in the profile.

    Killzones

    What it is. Shaded time windows (New York time): Asia 8pm to midnight, London 2am to 5am, New York morning 7am to 10am, London close 10am to noon.

    Effect. Most volume and the cleanest moves happen in these windows; outside them, moves are often choppy.

    How to use it. Prefer setups that form inside London or New York killzones. Be wary of signals in quiet hours.

    Pre-marketPre-market and after-hours

    What it is. Stock trading outside the regular session: pre-market from 4am to 9:30am and after-hours from 4pm to 8pm New York time. These candles are drawn faded, and the dashed orange line marks the latest pre-market or after-hours price, with its change from the last regular close under Price. Turn them off with the Pre and after hours box at the top. Daily candles never include them, but the dashed line still shows.

    Effect. Volume is thin, so prices can jump on a single headline and gaps between candles are common. A big pre-market move on news often sets the opening gap.

    How to use it. Read it as a preview of the open, not as a signal. The Entry Check, Timeframe Alignment, VWAP and the other markings use regular-hours candles only, so thin overnight prints don't create fake swings or zones.

    Trade plan (Entry Check)

    Long entryEntry zone and entry

    What it is. The gap or order block the setup would enter from (dashed blue box), and the suggested entry at its middle (solid blue line).

    Effect. Entering on the pullback instead of the breakout gives a tighter stop and better reward to risk.

    How to use it. "Forming" means wait for price to come back to the zone. "Ready" means price is in it now. The app never places the order; you decide.

    StopStop

    What it is. Just beyond the wick that swept liquidity, plus a small buffer (0.1 ATR).

    Effect. If price gets back past the sweep, the idea is wrong. A candle closing beyond the stop cancels the setup.

    How to use it. Size the position from the stop: decide how much you are willing to lose (for example 0.5% of your account), divide by the distance from entry to stop.

    Target 1Targets and reward to risk

    What it is. The next two untouched liquidity pools in the trade direction. Reward to risk (R) is the distance to target 1 divided by the distance to the stop.

    Effect. Price tends to travel from one pool of liquidity to the next.

    How to use it. The Entry Check needs at least 2R. Many traders take part profit at target 1 and move the stop to entry.

    formingreadyEntry Check score

    What it is. The checklist in the side panel, scored out of the steps that apply. The sweep, the structure break, the entry zone and at least 2R are required; the rest raise confidence.

    Effect. More checks lined up means more independent reasons agree, not a guarantee.

    How to use it. Read why a step failed (shown on the right of each line) and decide whether it matters for this trade.

    Side panel numbers

    Higher timeframe
    Structure on the bigger chart (15m checks 4h, 1h checks 1 day). Trades with it work more often than trades against it.
    ATR (14)
    Average candle range over the last 14 candles. Used to size gaps, stops and "how far is far".
    Relative volume
    This candle's volume against the average of the last 20. Above 1.5x means unusual activity.

    News, macro and confidence

    Confidence
    0 to 100 for each direction. Up to 60 points come from the Entry Check (how many chart checks pass), up to 40 from the news and macro context (how far it leans the same way), and up to 25 points come off when earnings or a Fed decision is days away. With no setup on the chart it stays at 40 or below. 70+ is high, 50 to 69 medium, under 50 low. It never tells you to trade: it tells you how much the evidence agrees.
    10-year and 2-year yields
    What the US government pays to borrow. Rising yields make future profits worth less today, which weighs hardest on tech, growth stocks, small caps and crypto. The 2-year follows what traders expect the Fed to do.
    Yield curve
    The 10-year minus the 2-year. Below zero (inverted) has often come before slowdowns. Shown for context, not scored.
    Fed decision (FOMC)
    Eight meetings a year set US interest rates. The statement lands at 2 pm New York time and price often swings both ways for an hour or more, so entries just before it carry extra risk.
    VIX
    The market's expected volatility for the S&P 500. Under 15 is calm, over 25 is fear, when stops get hit more and moves run further.
    US dollar
    A rising dollar tightens money worldwide and tends to weigh on crypto and on companies that sell abroad.
    Earnings and EPS
    Earnings per share against what analysts expected. A beat with raised guidance tends to lift the shares for weeks; a miss can gap them through any stop. The app flags reports within 7 days.
    Headline tone
    Recent Google News headlines scored from finance keywords (beats, downgrade, tariffs, rate cut…). It is rough and weighted low; read the stories it links.
    Politics and policy
    Tariffs, export controls, budget fights, regulation and elections. They arrive in the macro headlines, and Deep research reads them in depth.
    Deep research
    Optional. Claude searches the web, reads the sources and writes a cited bull and bear case with upcoming catalysts. It counts as the heaviest context input for an hour.
